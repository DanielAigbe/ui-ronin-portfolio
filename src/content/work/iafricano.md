## The problem

Big social platforms treat African content as one more category. The product brief describes iAfricano as the opposite: "a purpose-built space where being African is the default, not an afterthought." It is for Africans on the continent who want to share their culture, and for people in the diaspora who want to stay close to their roots.

The client gave me their product requirements document and asked for one thing: the app design, based on that document.

## My role

I worked from the client's requirements document, flagged the gaps in it, set up the design tokens in Tokens Studio, and designed the mobile app: onboarding, sign-up, login and password recovery, the home feed and posts, explore, communities, collections, profiles, chats, notifications and settings. I also built interactive HTML mockups to test the flows.

## Key decisions

- **Heritage is part of the profile, not a hidden setting.** People pick the countries that make up their heritage and the languages they speak. Both show on their profile as flags and tags, and a small country tag sits next to their name on posts and in chats.
- **No country filter on the home feed.** Filter pills were the first idea, but Africa has 54 countries and the row would be unusable. Browsing by country sits in Explore instead.
- **Posts can be shared with heritage connections only.** Besides Public and People I follow, there is a third audience: people who share your heritage. This was not in the original requirements; I added it while designing the post composer.
- **One reference library for every sheet, dialog and toast.** 24 numbered patterns, from post menus to delete confirmations, kept apart from the screens so each one is designed once and reused.
